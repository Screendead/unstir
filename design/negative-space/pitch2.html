Four ways to redraw everything outside the tank

You asked for a complete redesign of everything outside the tank, so it feels less like an app and more like a place. It had to be flat (2D, or layered at most), with no boxes, lists or straight lines, and nothing that stirs. Still Water stays as the restrained option; three new directions go much further. Every screen is drawn over the real glass, which is untouched, and no text is smaller than today. Each would run as one drawing pass that freezes while you play.

The call

recommended

The Descent

The game is one column of water seen from the side. The surface and today's daily are at the top; each tier is a deeper, darker band of water; endless glows in the abyss. Levels are lanterns on a slow current; packs drift past as portholes; settings are buoys on the surface. You dive to go on and rise to go back.

build
about 9–11 days
risk
medium; the most to build, and settings and the tier view have to be found, not seen

Ink

Everything is ink in still water, set like a beautiful page in New York italic. Words arrive as a drop that blooms and sharpens, and leave by dissolving. Solved levels read as a paragraph, the next one stands large, locked ones are haze that turns into words as you solve. Settings is a short letter: "Music is on."

build
about 5 days
risk
low-medium; words as buttons need to read as tappable

The Basin

You look straight down into a calm round pool. Levels are droplets resting on a current that spirals to the drain; you drag along it and they glide. The daily is the moon reflected on the water, endless is the drain, packs float like lily pads, settings rise as ripple rings.

build
about 6–8 days
risk
medium-high; only the next level is named, so finding an old one takes a glide

Still Water

Kept from the first round. Today's app, calmed: one thing in focus at a time, the rest one quiet line, colour as a glow behind the focus, everything settling slowly into place. Still menus and cards, just with room to breathe.

build
about 3 days
risk
low; it still feels like an app

Why The Descent

It is the only one where the game's own structure becomes the place: harder tiers are literally deeper, darker water, so progress is something you see and travel through rather than a list you scroll. Every screen belongs to one world (the surface, the dive, the porthole you play in), which is what makes it feel like an experience instead of an app. It is one flat, layered drawing pass, drawn once and frozen while a level plays, so the glass keeps its full budget and the battery cost at rest is about that of a dim live wallpaper.

One change I'd make: the mock-up relabels levels by depth ("14 m", and whirlpool 01 as "28 m"). Levels should keep their own numbers, with depth carried by the water's colour, so nothing players are told stops matching.

If you want the same idea for less: Ink is the most legible and the cheapest at about 5 days, and scales best to large text. If you do nothing, the chrome ships as it is today, and the App Store screenshots and preview film are retaken on today's look.

What the player would lose or see change

Picking an option approves its list. Prices, "free", "restore purchases" and every lock reason stay in all four.

The Descent

  • The tier capsules go: the opening view shows the whole dive, and you tap a tier's water to go there.
  • Levels become lanterns on a current; "3 rods · par 1" shows only on the next level; locked levels are dark lanterns.
  • The result marks become small bubbles; "at par", "moves 9 · par 9" and "new best" become one line, "at par · new best, was +6".
  • "Up a notch" becomes "deeper: whirlpool ›"; "‹ levels" becomes "↑ levels".
  • The dock's heading goes; covers drift as portholes; "new" becomes a dot.
  • The gear becomes a buoy; settings lose their explanation lines and read "on" or "off" under each buoy.
  • Prism's shop page loses its "pack · paid add-on" label, the "for / free / holds" keys and the tray headings; its specimens become 30 small lights.

Ink

  • Rows become a paragraph of level names with their marks as ink dots; only the next level shows rods and par.
  • Locked level names become unreadable haze until they open.
  • The tier capsules go: a swipe moves between tiers, the next one waiting at the page's edge.
  • The daily, endless and archive become sentences: "Today's daily, four days running." "Endless, best nine." "The archive comes with any pack."
  • The gear becomes the word "settings"; settings become a letter ("Music is on.") and lose their explanation lines.
  • All capsule buttons become words with a soft brushed underline; "moves 9 · par 9" goes from the result.
  • Prism's shop page loses its label and "for / free / holds" keys; its level list becomes one line.

The Basin

  • Only the next level and its two neighbours are named; other levels are droplets, locked ones dim specks.
  • Tier names ride the rim; vortex and maelstrom are a drag along the rim away.
  • The daily loses its date ("daily"); the archive drops "30 past dailies"; endless becomes the drain.
  • The dock's heading and cover names go; "new" becomes a dot.
  • The result's moves line goes; marks become motes; "up a notch" becomes "whirlpool ›" on a droplet.
  • Prism's shop page loses its level names: its specimens become 30 dots round the clip.
  • Settings become ripple rings ("music · on") and lose every explanation line.

Still Water

  • The menu leads with one large item and "play ›"; everything else is one quiet line; level numbers lose their colours.
  • Titles move to SF Pro; numbers stay mono.
  • The result keeps "at par" and "new best"; the clean hint waits behind a tap on the empty mark.
  • Capsule buttons, divider lines, the dock's heading and settings' explanation lines go.

Reduce Motion and the largest text

Under Reduce Motion each world is still: no drifting light, no bloom, no ripples; things fade in. At the third accessibility text size the text keeps its size and the layout and tank make room.
