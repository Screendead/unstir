Three ways to build The Descent

You picked The Descent: the game as one column of water seen from the side, with the daily at the surface, each tier a deeper, darker band and endless at the bottom. Here are three distinct versions of it. All three are flat or layered, with no boxes, lists or stirring. The glass is untouched, no text is smaller than today, and levels keep their own numbers ("14 Lather"), not depths in metres. Each would run as one drawing pass that is held still while a level plays.

The call

The Plumb Line

The sparest version. One hair-fine line hangs from a float at the surface, with a small weight at its tip that is always where you are. Levels are beads on the line, the next one largest. The water's colour alone carries the tiers, with thin white type and wide empty water. Packs hang on their own side lines, and settings are small floats at the surface.

build
about 8–10 days
risk
medium: the level view is the closest of the three to a list with a line through it, and thin type on the mid-teal shallows has the lowest contrast
recommended

Lamplight

The water is dark, and you carry one warm lamp, which sits at your next level and lights it and the two after it. Every level you have solved stays lit behind you as a small glow in its tier's colour, so your progress is a trail of light you can look back up at. The surface is at dusk; packs are distant glows; settings are lamps afloat on the waterline.

build
about 8–10 days
risk
medium: dark screens can show banding and are harder to read in sunlight; levels beyond the lamp show no name

The Shelf

The most illustrated version, with depth from parallax layers. A sand slope falls away beside the water in soft overhung ledges. Each level is a pebble on its own ledge, and solved pebbles catch the light. Each tier is its own colour of rock. Packs are lit hollows in the slope, and settings are stones on the first ledges under the surface.

build
about 10–12 days
risk
medium-high: the most to build and the longest scroll; packs no longer appear on the opening screen, which could cost sales

Why Lamplight

It adds the one thing the other two lack: your history is drawn into the place. Each solve leaves a light, so coming back to the menu shows how far down you have come, and a new tier means swimming into water you have never lit. The dark water also gives the glass the strongest stage of the three in play: the tank's neon is the brightest thing on screen. It costs about the same as The Plumb Line and less than The Shelf.

Two fixes I'd make while building it. Every level's name stays readable on request (tap a dim one and it names itself and its lock reason, and VoiceOver reads them all), so the dark never hides information. The ground is dithered so the near-black doesn't band on the phone.

If you'd rather keep everything readable at a glance, The Plumb Line is the calmest and most legible of the three. If you do nothing, the build waits, and the App Store screenshots and preview film wait with it.

What the player would lose or see change

All three share these changes from today:

  • The tier tabs become bands of water you tap or dive into.
  • Coloured level numbers become plain.
  • Capsule buttons, divider lines and the gear icon go.
  • The gradient UNSTIR becomes plain letter-spaced type.
  • "‹ levels" becomes "↑ levels".
  • "n rods · par n" shows only on the next level (except in The Shelf).

Prices, "free", "restore purchases", the play hint and every lock reason stay word for word in all three. Picking a version approves its list.

The Plumb Line

  • Lock icons become hollow beads on dimmed rows.
  • The daily, archive and endless are only on the whole-column view, not beside the levels.
  • The pack strip becomes packs on side lines at their own depths; its caption is kept on the whole-column view. First Stir isn't shown.
  • The result merges "at par" and "new best" into one line: "at par · new best, was +6".
  • The shop page loses its "pack · paid add-on" label, its "for / free / holds" labels and its level list. The 30 specimens become dots, with no names or per-tray progress.
  • Settings lose the explanation line under each switch.
  • About seven levels fit on a screen, against nine today.

Lamplight

  • Lock icons go: a locked level is a dark mote with no name until the lamp is within two levels of it. Locked tiers keep their written reason.
  • The pack strip and its caption become glows at depths along the dive. First Stir isn't shown.
  • The result merges "at par" and "new best" into one line, and the "moves 9 · par 9" line goes. "Up a notch" becomes "deeper".
  • The shop page loses its "pack · paid add-on" label, the "for: after plughole · reading by colour" line and its level list. The specimens become five shoals of lights.
  • Settings lose the explanation line under each switch.
  • About eight levels fit on a screen, against nine today.

The Shelf

  • The packs leave the opening screen: they are found only by diving. Their caption moves under plughole's name.
  • Lock icons become dull pebbles and dimmer text.
  • "n rods · par n" stays on every level.
  • The result keeps "at par", the moves line and "new best".
  • The shop page loses its "for / free / holds" labels and its level list. The 30 specimens become pebbles, and a new line reads "0/30 at par".
  • Settings keep every explanation line.
  • About eight levels fit on a screen, and a tier is a long dive.

Reduce Motion and the largest text

Under Reduce Motion nothing drifts or sways: moves become cuts and things fade in. At the third accessibility text size the text keeps its size and the layout and tank make room. These stills cover the dive and play; the other screens at that size come with the build.

What happens next

On your pick, that version is built in the app screen by screen. Each screen comes back to you as screenshots and films from the phone before it merges. You can also take one version and borrow a single piece of another, for example Lamplight with The Shelf's settings, which keep their explanation lines. The App Store screenshots and preview film are retaken after this.
