Unstir is a puzzle game made by Jack Lusher, its developer ("we", "us"). This page says what the game keeps, what it
sends, to whom, and what you can do about it. Jack Lusher is the controller of the data described here. Contact:
`{{CONTACT_ADDRESS}}`.

## In short

- Your progress stays on your phone. There is no account and no sign-in.
- If you are 13 or over, the game asks you once whether it may send counts of how you play. Nothing is sent unless you
  say yes, and you can change your answer at any time in settings.
- If you are under 13, or your App Store country is in the EU or the EEA, the game never asks and never sends anything.
- It never sends your name, your Apple ID, your contacts, your photos, your precise location or any lasting identifier
  for you, your phone or your install.
- There are no ads, no tracking across apps or websites, and no data sold or shared with advertisers.

## What stays on your phone

The game keeps these on your phone only. They never leave it except as described below:
- Your progress: best scores, which levels you have started, the tier the menu shows, endless's best run.
- The undo bank and the day it last refilled. To keep the refill fair when the phone's clock is changed, the game also
  keeps the last time it trusted, the phone's time since it started up, and an identifier of that start-up that the
  system provides. These are used only on the phone.
- Which lessons you have learnt (turning the tank, using the swirl).
- Your answer to the age question and to the analytics question.
- Only after you say yes to analytics: a short tally used to build the summaries described below. Before you answer,
  and after a no, the game keeps no tally at all.
- A log of recent play (the last 3,000 lines: where each touch landed and moved, each move, undo and reset, levels
  opened, failed purchases, and when the app started). It is kept for finding bugs and leaves the phone only if you
  send it yourself.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. If your phone backs up to iCloud or a computer, the backup includes it, under your
backup's own terms.

## Purchases

Packs and Complete are sold through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment, and we never see your payment details or
Apple ID. The game checks Apple's signed record of what you own on the phone, with no server of ours. Apple's privacy
policy covers Apple's part.

## If you say yes to analytics

The game sends counts of how you play to Aptabase, an analytics service (Technov Solutions SRL, Romania), on its EU
region: the data is processed and stored in the EU. Aptabase acts for us as our processor, under its data processing
agreement; its sub-processors are Amazon Web Services (hosting) and Tinybird (its analytics database), listed in that
agreement at aptabase.com/legal/dpa. We use the counts to see whether the game is clear, where players get stuck and
whether they come back. What is sent:

- That you said yes, and on each later day you open the game, which day it is, counted from the day you said yes (day
  2, day 3, ...).
- For each level you finish, lose or leave: which level (every daily is sent as "daily"), whether you solved it, lost
  it or left, your moves, its par, and the undos and resets you used. For an endless run: how it ended, the tanks
  cleared and the moves. Nothing is sent for the sandbox.
- Once each: that you saw a pack's card, finished its free levels, or bought a pack or Complete on this phone (the
  product's name only, no price or receipt).
- On days 2, 7 and 30, one summary: which days you played since saying yes, undos used and banked, endless's best, the
  furthest level in each tier, levels started, dailies solved first time, and for each pack whether you saw it,
  finished its free levels and own it.
- With each event: its time, a random number for the current sitting (renewed after an hour without play), whether the
  build is a development build, and the app's version. No device model, system version or language.

Every request reaches Aptabase from your internet address, as any request to a server does. Aptabase uses the address
and the request's software description, in memory only, to note your country and region (for example a state or
"England"), and to make a daily pseudonymous number from them with a key it changes every 24 hours and deletes within
48 hours. It doesn't store the address. Aptabase keeps the events for up to 5 years (6 months for development builds).

**Legal basis.** Your consent (UK GDPR article 6(1)(a)).

**Changing your mind.** Saying no, or turning analytics off in settings, stops all sending at once, deletes anything
waiting to be sent and deletes the tally on the phone. Your progress is not affected. Saying yes again starts the count
afresh.

**Your rights.** You can withdraw consent at any time, as above. Nothing we receive names you, your phone or your
install, so we can't pick out your events from anyone else's. Under UK GDPR article 11, where we can't identify you,
the rights to access, correct, delete or move your data apply only if you give us information that lets us identify
your events; tell us at `{{CONTACT_ADDRESS}}` and we will help as far as we can.

## If you send a log

Settings has a "send log" button. It opens your phone's share sheet with the play log described above, and you choose
where it goes, for example an email to us. The game sends nothing by itself. If you email it, we receive your email
address with it and use both only to look into the bug you reported. We keep the email until the report is dealt
with, then delete it. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in fixing the problem you reported (UK GDPR article
6(1)(f)).

## Sharing a result

The daily's share button opens your phone's share sheet with a line of text (the daily's number or date, your moves
and par, and a square per move). It goes only where you send it.

## Children

The game is for everyone, so before it asks about analytics it asks whether you are under 13 or 13 or over. If you are
under 13, it never asks about analytics and never sends anything, and it remembers the answer so it doesn't ask again.
A parent who thinks a child under 13 said yes can write to `{{CONTACT_ADDRESS}}`; turning analytics off in settings
stops it at once.

## Where you are

We are based in the United Kingdom, and UK data protection law covers everything described here. On App Store
storefronts in the EU and the EEA the game sends no analytics at all, so for players there it keeps data only on the
phone, and we receive only the emails they choose to send.

## Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, write to `{{CONTACT_ADDRESS}}` first: we acknowledge a complaint
within 30 days and reply as soon as we can. You can also complain to the UK regulator, the Information Commission
(formerly the Information Commissioner's Office), at ico.org.uk, or to the data protection authority where you live.
